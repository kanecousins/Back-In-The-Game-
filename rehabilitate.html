Step 3

Rehabilitate

Four criteria-based phases. Per phase: goals and exercises, then outcome measures. Move on when every measure is ticked. Most gates come from two protocols and clinical judgement, not validated trials (see About).

Phase Goal Move on when
1. Settle & activate Settle symptoms and restore daily function; learn neutral-spine trunk control All 6 measures met
2. Build capacity Build trunk endurance (60-second holds) and flexion strength; build lower-body and hinge strength 2–3 times per week All 7 measures met
3. Sport-specific reintroduction Return to extension and rotation, step by step; rebuild sport technique and workload All 6 measures met
4. Full training & competition Tolerate full training without a reaction; meet all three return to sport checklists All 6 measures met

Principles

  • Rest from sport, not from rehabilitation. Start guided exercise early.
  • Criteria, not dates. Protocols give week ranges but progress by criteria.
  • No routine brace (about 83% improved with or without; the trial used none). Follow the treating doctor.
  • One variable at a time. Judge a session by the next morning.
  • Build capacity beyond sport demand. Pain-free is not the finish line. Progress strength toward the loads the sport puts through the back and hips, or reaggravation on return is more likely. (Clinical judgement.)

Pain rules and flare-ups

  • Phases 1–2: judge by symptom response: no increase in symptoms during the session or the next morning, not a fixed 0/10 target.
  • Phases 3–4: up to 2/10 if it settles within 24 hours.
  • Mild flare (to 2/10, settles in 24 h): reduce the dose.
  • Moderate (3–5/10 or over 24–48 h): pause the task, return to the last pain-free phase, look for the cause.
  • Severe, persistent or new neurological symptoms: stop and escalate.

The trial in one line

Athletes aged 10–19 with MRI-confirmed active spondylolysis who started physiotherapy within 7 days returned at a median of 74 vs 112 days, with 3% vs 29% recurrence (Selhorst et al., 2026; n = 64, two US centres, unblinded). Do not generalise.
