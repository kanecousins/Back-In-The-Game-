About

Helps new-graduate physios recognise, escalate and manage lumbar pars stress injuries in adolescent athletes.

Why it is needed

  • The presentation is non-specific and no single clinical test is reliable.
  • Early injury can be missed on X-ray.
  • Pars defects are common without symptoms.
  • Existing protocols and the trial target experienced clinicians, and differ on timing, bracing and return criteria.
  • In a pragmatic (not systematic) review, no source combined clinical reasoning, communication and return criteria for new graduates.

Evidence map

Strength of basis for key recommendations. Most phase gates come from protocols, other injuries or clinical judgement.

Recommendation Strength of basis Source
Start physiotherapy early Stronger: one RCT (n = 64), specific population Selhorst et al. (2026)
Pain-free motion, 2 weeks pain-free return activity, Micheli 0% Stronger: RCT criteria Selhorst et al. (2026)
No single test reliable Moderate: systematic review Alqarni et al. (2015)
Phase gates (holds, lowering, jog) Protocol; not validated Mass General Brigham (2024); Sanford Health (2024); Brearley et al. (2021)
Indicator count, pain rules Clinical judgement This resource
Strength progression Indirect: narrative review of youth strength training (safe, effective, may increase bone density); not specific to spondylolysis Stakeholder feedback; Dahab & McCambridge (2009)

Not yet known: best dose and progression; a validated return battery and psychological threshold; whether the trial applies to chronic defects, bilateral lesions, vertebral slips or other settings; long-term outcomes of non-union.

Limits

  • A student-developed educational project, not a clinical practice guideline. Does not replace supervision, local protocols or the treating doctor.
  • This resource collates the available information on spondylolysis, but there is no gold standard for deciding when an athlete is ready to move to the next phase. The phase gates are based on limited and confounded research, protocols and clinical judgement, so treat them as guides, not validated cut-offs. Check Physiotherapy Board of Australia and APA guidance and local pathways.
