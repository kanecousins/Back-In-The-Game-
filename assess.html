Step 2

Assess & Refer

Red flags: escalate urgently

Night pain unrelated to position, fever or weight loss, neurological deficit or saddle anaesthesia, significant trauma.

Imaging is not the first step

Clinical diagnosis supported by imaging. Discuss referral with an experienced physiotherapist and the medical team, and plan how you will explain the result first.

Examine: build a pattern, not a verdict

No single test is reliable. Stork test (best of 34): sensitivity 50–73%, specificity 0–87% (Alqarni et al., 2015).

Test Reading
Neuro screen Deficit means urgent review
Extension, extension–rotation (standing, quadrant) Familiar pain raises suspicion
Stork test Positive raises suspicion; negative does not exclude
Sport-specific movement Familiar pain adds to pattern
Hip and lumbopelvic screen Not diagnostic; sets rehab targets

What else could it be?

Pattern Consider

Groupings from More & John (2014). Also ask about load, growth, family history and, sensitively, bone health and energy availability (Mountjoy et al., 2023).

A reasoning aid, not a validated rule. Discuss with an experienced physiotherapist if you are still unsure.

Imaging: discuss before you refer
  • X-ray: sensitivity about 33%; can miss early injury
  • MRI: shows oedema; some false negatives first-line
  • CT: best cortical detail; radiation in the young

McDonald et al. (2023); Brearley et al. (2021). Do not scan to confirm healing: it is not a trial return criterion.

How to word your findings

Avoid Prefer
“You have a broken spine.” “This is a stress injury in a growing bone from repeated load. It is common in young athletes, and we know how to help it recover.”
“It’s nothing, just a scan finding.” “Your pain is real. The finding may be part of the explanation, and we will use how you move and feel alongside it.”
“It may never heal.” “It may or may not fully join, and many people recover and return to sport either way.”

Clinician messages shape back beliefs for years (Darlow et al., 2013). Validate, state what is known and unknown, and agree one first step.

When things do not go to plan
Situation Action
Pain not settling Check dose, technique, unplanned load, sleep and stress, beliefs. Re-examine and review the diagnosis with an experienced physiotherapist. One protocol repeats imaging only if still symptomatic at about 12 weeks (Sanford, 2024).
Bilateral defects Higher slip risk: involve the sports physician early
Chronic defect or late presentation Defect may be incidental: manage by symptoms and function, keep looking for other sources
Slip on imaging Specialist grading first; urgent referral if high grade
New neurological symptoms Stop and escalate urgently
No progress after about 6 months of structured care Surgical review. Pooled surgical return: 88% at any level, mean 6.8 months (Grazina et al., 2019)
